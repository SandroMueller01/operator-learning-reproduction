\chapter{Documented Deviations from the Paper}
\label{app:an-appendix}

Most of the details that once needed to be reconstructed here (the mesh,
the NSB finite element family, the inlet velocity, the pressure gauge, the
sparse-grid level) turned out to be available in the paper authors' own
released code, and this reproduction now matches all of them exactly, as
already discussed in Chapter~\ref{sec:methodology} and revisited in
Chapter~\ref{sec:experimentalresults} and Chapter~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
What remains here are the two genuine, deliberate deviations from the
paper that this reproduction still makes.

\begin{description}
  \item[Deep learning framework (Section~\ref{sec:problemStatement}).]
  The paper's reference implementation uses TensorFlow/Keras. This project
  substitutes PyTorch and JAX, run side by side, as a deliberate project
  constraint rather than an oversight. The PyTorch/JAX framework
  comparison in Section~\ref{sec:jaximplementation} is itself part of the
  intended deliverable. Because Keras' \texttt{HeUniform} initializer is
  activation-agnostic, both reimplementations had to be adjusted so that
  their own (activation-aware) default Kaiming/Xavier conventions matched
  Keras' behavior instead (Section~\ref{sec:pytorchreimplementation}).

  \item[NSB training epoch budget (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).]
  The paper's stated training protocol, up to 60{,}000 epochs, stopping
  once the training loss falls below an absolute tolerance of
  $5\times10^{-7}$, is used unchanged for the diffusion experiment, where
  most runs converge well before the epoch cap. For NSB it is not: direct
  measurement (one task's loss trace showed $6.5\times10^{-6}$, still
  decreasing but far from the tolerance, after 11{,}000 of 60{,}000 epochs)
  showed that a large fraction of NSB runs, particularly the larger
  architecture and larger training-set sizes, need tens of thousands of
  epochs under the exponentially-decaying learning rate to approach
  $5\times10^{-7}$, and many effectively consume the full 60{,}000-epoch
  budget. Timed directly on this machine, the full NSB paper matrix
  (16{,}128 training runs) at that budget projected to roughly nine days of
  wall-clock time even with 16-way process parallelism, which is not
  tractable within the scope of this practical work. For the NSB experiment
  only, the epoch cap is reduced to 20{,}000 and the loss tolerance loosened
  to $5\times10^{-6}$ (configs/train/pytorch\_nsb.yaml,
  configs/train/jax\_nsb.yaml). Every other element of the training
  protocol (optimizer, learning-rate schedule, checkpoint/restore rule,
  batch size, per-trial seeding) is unchanged. This is applied uniformly
  across all twelve trials of every NSB configuration, so that the
  geometric-mean/std statistics reported in
  Chapter~\ref{sec:experimentalresults} are not a mix of two different
  training budgets within the same cell.
\end{description}
