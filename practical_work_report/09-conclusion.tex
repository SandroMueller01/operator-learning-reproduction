\chapter{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\section{Discussion}

The reproduction succeeded in the sense that matters for this practical
work: not bit-exact agreement with the paper's plotted curves, which is not
achievable given the TensorFlow/Keras~$\to$~PyTorch/JAX framework
substitution that predates this report, but agreement in the paper's
qualitative claims. Across both the Hilbert-valued diffusion experiment and
the Banach-valued NSB experiment, the reproduced error decays at
approximately the paper's reported $O(m^{-1})$ rate (fitted slopes between
$-0.91$ and $-1.06$ across all cases and both output targets), and
ELU/tanh architectures beat ReLU by a wide, consistent margin everywhere --
the single most robust finding in the whole study. The claim of no
degradation from $d=4$ to $d=8$ is the one place the reproduction diverges
from a clean match: it holds for the architectures the paper itself
prefers (ELU/tanh) but not for ReLU, and, interestingly, the specific
property that predicts robustness differs between the two PDEs
(activation for diffusion, architecture size for NSB;
Section~\ref{sec:experimentalresults}). This is reported as a genuine
empirical finding rather than reconciled away, since it is consistent
across coefficient families within each PDE.

PyTorch and JAX agree with each other closely under the identical protocol
described in Section~\ref{sec:jaximplementation}: relative test error
differs by well under 1.5\% between frameworks in every one of the twelve
cases (Section~\ref{sec:framework-comparison-results}). This close
agreement is itself evidence that any remaining mismatch with the paper's
own reported numbers comes from the framework substitution and the
documented data-generation deviations (Appendix~\ref{app:an-appendix}),
rather than from an error specific to this reproduction's own pipeline --
if the pipeline itself were unreliable, two independently implemented
frameworks trained on identical data would have no particular reason to
agree this closely. Training time is a different story: JAX is faster for
diffusion in every case (9--28\%), but the NSB results are mixed, with
PyTorch occasionally faster -- consistent with the sweep's many distinct
architecture/training-size shape combinations eating into JAX's
JIT-compilation advantage, an effect anticipated in
Section~\ref{sec:jaximplementation} before the full sweep was run.

\section{Limitations}

The stationary Boussinesq equation, the paper's third experiment, was out
of scope for this practical work (Section~\ref{sec:problemStatement}). The
NSB finite element family and inlet/pressure conventions were reconstructed
from the paper's cited source and calibrated against the paper's reported
figures rather than read off an explicit formula stated in the paper itself
(Section~\ref{sec:methodology}). The diffusion mesh is structured rather
than the unstructured mesh the paper's own figures suggest, due to a
tooling incompatibility (Appendix~\ref{app:an-appendix}). The sparse-grid
test level was chosen empirically rather than taken from a value stated in
the paper. NSB training additionally uses a reduced epoch budget and a
looser loss tolerance than the paper specifies
(Appendix~\ref{app:an-appendix}), a deviation made necessary by measured
wall-clock cost rather than by any property of the paper's method itself;
Section~\ref{sec:experimentalresults} shows this most plausibly explains
NSB's slightly shallower fitted decay rate relative to diffusion, and it
means the NSB numbers in this report are not run under a strictly
paper-identical training budget the way the diffusion numbers are.
Finally, all absolute error magnitudes in this report are specific to the
PyTorch/JAX implementations and the mesh/element choices described above;
they should not be read as literal reproductions of the paper's own
plotted values, only as a check on the paper's qualitative claims.

\section{Conclusion}

Both PyTorch and JAX reproductions of the diffusion and NSB experiments
recover the paper's central qualitative findings -- an approximately
$m^{-1}$ error-decay rate and a clear activation-function ranking -- using
the paper's own mixed finite element data-generation pipeline and
sparse-grid test protocol rather than a simplified stand-in, at the paper's
full experimental scale (2 PDEs, 2 coefficient families, 2 dimensions, 6
architectures, 14 training sizes, 12 trials, both frameworks: 24,094
completed training runs (7,966 diffusion, 16,128 NSB) with zero failures).

Implementing the paper's protocol precisely rather than approximately
turned out to matter in several concrete ways that a looser reproduction
would have missed entirely: the diffusion mixed formulation's natural
(rather than essential) boundary condition changes what "implementing the
boundary condition" even means at the code level; Keras'
activation-agnostic \texttt{HeUniform} initializer is a silent, easy-to-miss
mismatch against both PyTorch's and JAX's own activation-aware defaults;
and the paper's two-trigger checkpoint/restore rule (best-loss-so-far
\emph{or} an eighth-ratio drop) is meaningfully different from, and not
reducible to, a simple best-loss tracker. Each of these was only caught by
implementing the letter of the paper's appendix rather than a plausible
approximation of it. Recovering the paper's qualitative claims despite
these implementation-level departures from a naive reading of the method
description suggests the paper's reported behavior is a robust property of
the method, not an artifact of one specific framework or one specific
finite element choice.

The most natural next step for extending this reproduction is adding the
stationary Boussinesq equation, the paper's third experiment, which was
excluded from this practical work's scope
(Section~\ref{sec:problemStatement}) but would complete the paper's full
experimental picture using the same infrastructure built here. A second
natural extension is re-running the NSB experiment under the paper's full,
un-reduced training budget (Appendix~\ref{app:an-appendix}) given more
compute time than was available for this practical work, to check whether
NSB's slightly shallower fitted decay rate closes the small remaining gap
to diffusion's.
